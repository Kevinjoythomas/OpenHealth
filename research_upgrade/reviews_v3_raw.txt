## 4. The single highest-leverage fix

**Fix M1 — the conditional-vs-joint error in the headline "68% vs 14%" statistic.** It is the highest-leverage change for three reasons: (1) it is the paper's flagship number, propagated through the Abstract, §1, §4.3, §4.5, §4.7, and §4.2, so one correct definition repairs six locations at once; (2) it is a genuine defect, not a matter of taste — the base is wrong by ~3x — and it is the kind of error that, once a reviewer finds it, poisons trust in every other number in the paper; (3) fixing it *narrows* the base-vs-fine-tune contrast (75% vs 40% conditional, or the joint reading), which is precisely the credibility signal the paper needs — it demonstrates the authors will report the corrected, less-flattering number.
