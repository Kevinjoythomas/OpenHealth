description

summarize Abstract

Deploying clinical LLMs in low-resource settings is constrained by compute cost, privacy risk, and unsafe hallucinations. OpenHealth addresses these limits with a 4-bit quantised LLaMA 3 8B model, LoRA adaptation, and confidence-gated hybrid retrieval. The full pipeline runs locally on an RTX 3050 Laptop GPU with 8 GB VRAM while improving unseen-case ROUGE-1 by 26.7% and raising the clinical rubric score from 6.1/8 to 7.2/8.

Methodology

neurology

1. Base Model Selection

LLaMA 3 8B was chosen for its strong instruction-following baseline and stable performance under low-memory deployment constraints.

memory

2. 4-bit Quantisation

NF4 4-bit quantisation via bitsandbytes compresses the model to roughly 5.4 GB VRAM, enabling operation on an 8 GB laptop GPU.

hub

3. Hybrid RAG Pipeline

BM25 and nomic-embed-text retrieval run in parallel, are fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, and only pass context when the score exceeds 0.020.

tune

4. LoRA Fine-Tuning

Rank-8 LoRA adapters were trained on 112,165 ChatDoctor consultations, updating only 0.26% of total model parameters.

Key Findings

  • check_circle ROUGE is the main quality signal: the fine-tuned model reaches ROUGE-1 = 0.327 on unseen cases, outperforming both its base generalisation score and its memorisation split.
  • check_circle Phrase-level improvement is substantial: ROUGE-2 improves by 94.3%, showing stronger acquisition of medical phrasing and clinician-style response patterns.
  • check_circle Clinical checks still matter: rubric score and safety recovery remain on the poster because ROUGE alone does not capture urgent-referral behaviour.
Conclusion: OpenHealth shows that clinically grounded QA does not require datacentre hardware. Quantised local inference, low-rank adaptation, and confidence-gated hybrid retrieval deliver safer, faster answers while keeping patient data on-device.
